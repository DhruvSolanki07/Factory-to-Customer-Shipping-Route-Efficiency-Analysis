Abstract

National multi-echelon consumer packaged goods (CPG) distribution networks face complex trade-offs among multi-facility fulfillment, carrier service classes, geographic dispatch density, and delivery lead-time variance. This investigation analyzes nationwide factory-to-customer logistics patterns for Nassau Candy Distributor using a comprehensive transactional dataset encompassing 10,194 line items, 8,549 distinct customer purchase orders, 5,044 unique accounts, and five specialized confectionery production nodes across the continental United States. We formulate distinct order-level routing graphs, quantify throughput concentrations across four geographic macro-regions (Pacific, Atlantic, Interior, Gulf), examine fulfillment mode utilization across four service levels, and establish an algorithmic framework for factory-to-destination routing efficiency.

Critically, our exploratory data audit uncovers a systemic temporal paradox in recorded dispatch timestamps (Order Dates: 2024–2025; Ship Dates: 2026–2030), yielding unverified transit durations of 904–1,642 days (median: 1,274 days). Rather than uncritically treating these legacy testing artifacts as ground truth, we architect a dual-track analytical protocol: (1) an end-to-end topological and financial network optimization across all 8,549 verified orders (\$141,783.63 aggregate sales; \$93,442.80 gross margin), and (2) an anomaly-isolated threshold validation model governing operational SLA claims. We conclude with actionable network rebalancing recommendations, dynamic multi-facility allocation heuristics, and a roadmap toward real-time GIS carrier tracking integration.

1. Introduction

In contemporary fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) and specialty candy supply chains, the synchronization between upstream manufacturing specialization and downstream demand geography represents the primary driver of fulfillment velocity, carrier freight expenditure, and client service satisfaction. Confectionery logistics introduces unique operational constraints, including SKU-level thermal vulnerabilities, seasonal inventory surges, and decentralized production facilities with non-interchangeable tooling.

Nassau Candy Distributor operates as a premier wholesale manufacturer and national distributor. Efficient fulfillment demands precise orchestration between specialized confectionery production plants and thousands of independent retail, grocery, and corporate customer destinations spanning every US state and Canadian province. This research paper formulates an empirical routing analysis of the Nassau Candy distribution network to address three fundamental research inquiries:

  • RQ1: Hub Allocation & Volume Disparity: How is nationwide dispatch volume distributed across specialized production hubs, and what structural concentration risks emerge from SKU-exclusive manufacturing assignments?
  • RQ2: Geographic Routing & Regional Topography: What are the primary destination corridors and geographic bottlenecks across customer states, and how do transcontinental distribution corridors impact logistics overhead?
  • RQ3: Data Integrity & Lead-Time Forensics: When corporate ERP databases exhibit temporal anomalies in milestone recording, how can enterprise analytics platforms maintain analytical integrity while isolating unverified transactional anomalies?

3. Temporal Integrity Audit & Forensic Analysis

An imperative requirement of data science in corporate logistics is auditing data provenance before accepting system metrics as operational truth. During initial exploratory examination, evaluating the delta between raw dispatch milestones exposed an extreme divergence:

Tlead = Dship − Dorder
Data Integrity Alert: In all 10,194 records, Order Dates span from 02-Jan-2024 to 31-Dec-2025, while recorded Ship Dates span from 30-Jun-2026 to 28-Jun-2030. This manifests in calculated transit durations of 904 to 1,642 calendar days (mean: 1,274 days; median: 1,274 days).

Forensic cross-referencing of order tracking keys reveals order IDs prefixed with historical fiscal markers (e.g., US-2021-103800, CA-2024-143500). This distribution pattern indicates that during synthetic benchmarking, ERP migration, or date scrubbing, shipping milestones were artificially forward-projected by 900+ days.

Architectural Safeguard: NassauLogix establishes an operational validity gate requiring 0 ≤ T_lead ≤ 365 days. Rather than suppressing the entire dataset or allowing false SLA failure claims to propagate to management dashboards, NassauLogix gracefully isolates lead-time measures while fully illuminating shipment counts, parcel weights, and financial metrics across all 8,549 authentic shipments.

4. Methodology & Mathematical Formulations

To evaluate logistics performance across regional routes, we define a suite of route-level metrics applied over any grouping tuple r = (Nodeorigin, Statedestination):

4.1 Distinct Order Parcel Volume

Because confectionery orders frequently contain multiple distinct confectionery lines, aggregating raw rows biases high-variety orders over single-bulk dispatches. We define route volume V(r) as the cardinality of unique order identifiers:

V(r) = |{ Order_IDi ∣ (Origini, Desti) = r }|

4.2 Route Delay Frequency

Given an SLA threshold parameter τ (standardized at 7 days in default operations), the route delay rate Pdelay(r) is formulated as the proportion of validated orders exceeding the threshold:

Pdelay(r) = (1 / |Or,valid|) × ∑ 1(Ti > τ)

4.3 Min-Max Normalized Efficiency Score

To benchmark relative route velocity on a standardized [0, 100] scale, we calculate normalized efficiency E(r) against the fleet-wide lead-time boundaries:

E(r) = 100 × [ max(L̄) − L̄(r) ] / [ max(L̄) − min(L̄) + ε ]

where L̄(r) denotes the average validated lead time on route r and ε = 10−9 prevents division by zero in homogeneous fleets.

6. Strategic Operational Recommendations

Based on topological network density, manufacturing bottlenecks, and multi-tier distribution requirements, we offer four strategic recommendations:

  1. Dual-Sourcing of Top-Tier Confectionery SKUs: 96.0% of total national volume is fulfilled exclusively from two facilities (Casa Grande, AZ at 55.7% and Savannah, GA at 40.3%). Cross-licensing chocolate bar manufacturing across both hubs would eliminate transcontinental cross-hauling to coastal markets.
  2. Consolidated Transshipment Hubs in California and Texas: California (1,686 orders) and Texas (833 orders) represent the largest single-state consumption sinks. Establishing regional cross-docking facilities would enable full truckload (FTL) long-haul transit followed by local last-mile courier delivery.
  3. Automated Carrier EDI / API Telemetry: Transition from static batch dates to electronic data interchange (EDI 214 shipment status messages) to capture carrier pickup, transit hub departure, and proof-of-delivery timestamps.
  4. Dynamic Service Tier Optimization: Identify non-urgent wholesale replenish orders erroneously booked under expedited First Class or Same Day, redirecting them to consolidated ground freight to reduce unnecessary transport expense.

7. Limitations & Future Research Scope

The present analysis was conducted under several real-world enterprise constraints:

  • Absence of Explicit Carrier Freight Rates: Accounting records capture wholesale selling prices and manufacturing costs, but lack line-haul, fuel surcharge, and parcel dimensional tariffs.
  • Euclidean vs. OSRM Road Distance: Geographic factory-to-state vectors use straight-line coordinate centroids rather than actual interstate highway routing networks (e.g., I-10, I-80 corridors).
  • Predictive Modeling Prerequisite: Machine learning models for lead-time delay prediction (e.g., XGBoost, LightGBM) should be trained exclusively after carrier EDI milestone feeds are authenticated.

8. Conclusion

The NassauLogix logistics intelligence project establishes a rigorous, scalable analytical architecture for evaluating factory-to-customer distribution efficiency in multi-facility confectionery supply chains. By identifying structural volume concentrations across Casa Grande and Savannah, categorizing regional order mix, and instituting an uncompromising data-integrity validation framework to prevent flawed reporting, this study delivers both immediate operational visibility and a robust foundation for automated logistics optimization.
